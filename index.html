Message From Warren E. Buffett, CEO of Berkshire Hathaway Inc.

Dear Reader,

I have spent most of my life studying businesses, markets and the behavior of investors. One thing has remained remarkably consistent: ultimately, markets are driven by attention. Money tends to follow it. For most of my career, that attention was directed toward companies, products, brands and industries, so investors looked at earnings, assets, competitive advantages and the ability of a business to generate cash over many years.

The internet has changed the speed at which attention moves. Today, millions of people can focus on the same idea within minutes. A person can post an image, a joke, a phrase or a simple concept, and suddenly millions of people know about it. In that environment, attention itself has become an economic force, which is one reason I find the phenomenon of memecoins interesting.

A memecoin may begin with little more than an image, a joke or a cultural reference. It may have no factory, no employees and no traditional business model. Yet, if enough people decide to pay attention to it, something unusual can happen: the attention becomes the asset. People begin talking about the coin, communities form around it, influential individuals mention it, and the media notices. Other people see those discussions and become interested, so the process can reinforce itself.

This does not mean that every memecoin has value. Quite the opposite. Most will disappear. But I would not make the mistake of assuming that something has no significance simply because it began as a joke. Markets have repeatedly demonstrated that human attention can create enormous economic consequences.

The important question is not always, "What does this asset produce?" Sometimes the more interesting question is: "How many people are paying attention to it, and why?"

Consider what happens when a particular idea becomes globally recognizable. A photograph, a symbol, a phrase, a character or a meme can travel across borders without requiring a factory, a distribution network or a television advertising campaign. If that idea becomes sufficiently powerful, a token associated with it can become a global representation of that attention. That is a very different phenomenon from traditional investing.

The stock market is ultimately a market for ownership in businesses. A memecoin is something different. Its price can be driven overwhelmingly by culture, community, speculation and attention. But attention should not be underestimated. The world has repeatedly placed enormous value on things whose usefulness was difficult to measure in conventional terms. Brands are valuable because people recognize them. Networks are valuable because people participate in them. Media companies are valuable because people watch them.

A token can, in certain circumstances, become valuable for a related reason: people care about it. Of course, there is an enormous difference between attention and lasting value. Attention can arrive quickly and disappear just as quickly. Anyone participating in this market should understand that.

Still, I believe the larger lesson is worth considering. The next generation of global assets may not always begin with a company executive, a factory or a financial institution. Some may begin with a meme, some with a community, and some with a single post that captures the imagination of millions of people.

In an increasingly connected world, there is no reason to assume that we already know what the world's next great symbol will be. A token that nobody notices today can, under extraordinary circumstances, become known everywhere tomorrow. That does not make it a good investment, but it makes the phenomenon impossible to ignore.

As I have often said in one form or another, it is wise to understand what you are buying. With memecoins, that means understanding that you may not be buying a traditional business at all. You may be buying a piece of collective attention, and attention, in the modern economy, can move surprisingly fast.

Sincerely,
Sincerely, Warren E. Buffett
